About the Campaign

Digital Dignity Campaign is a social action initiative created to raise awareness among people about online gender-based violence (OGBV) and to empower individuals with practical safety knowledge.
In today’s digital world, social media platforms have become part of everyday life. People share their experiences, education, daily life etc online. However, these platforms are not always safe. Many individuals especially women and girls face harassment, cyberstalking, online blackmail, hate speech, and non-consensual sharing of images.

Technology-facilitated gender-based violence is not “just online.” It affects mental health, confidence, privacy, and real-life safety.
This campaign focuses on:
1.Educating people about different forms of online abuse.
2.Providing practical and easy-to-follow safety tips.
3.Encouraging victims to report abuse.
4.Promoting respectful digital behavior.

Our goal is simple:
To create a safer, more respectful digital environment where everyone can participate without fear. Through awareness, education, and collective action, we believe digital spaces can become places of empowerment rather than harm.

What is Online Gender-Based Violence?

Online Gender-Based Violence (OGBV) refers to harmful actions carried out through digital platforms that target individuals based on their gender. It includes harassment, threats, abuse, stalking, and the sharing of private content without consent. This form of violence often occurs through social media, messaging apps, gaming platforms, and other online spaces.

Technology-facilitated violence can have serious emotional, psychological, and social consequences. It affects dignity, privacy, and personal safety.

Types of Online Gender Based Violence:

  1. Cyber harassment.
  2. Cyberstalking.
  3. Non-consensual image sharing.
  4. Online blackmail.
  5. Impersonation.

Safety Tips to counter Online Gender Based Violence

Protect Yourself in the Digital World

Online gender-based violence includes online harassment, cyberstalking, threats, blackmail, impersonation, and non-consensual sharing of private content. While the responsibility always lies with the abuser, taking preventive digital safety measures can help reduce risks and increase protection.
Below are practical and effective safety strategies:

1. Use Strong and Unique Passwords

Create passwords that are long, complex, and difficult to guess. Avoid using your name, birthdate, or common words.

  • Use at least 12 characters.
  • Combine letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Use different passwords for different accounts.
  • Consider using a password manager.

2.Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of security. Even if someone steals your password, they cannot access your account without a second verification code.
Activate 2FA on social media.
Enable it on email accounts.
Use authentication apps instead of SMS when possible.

3.Adjust Your Privacy Settings

Regularly review your privacy settings on social media platforms.

  • Make profiles private.
  • Limit who can see your posts.
  • Control who can send you messages.
  • Restrict unknown followers.
  • Do not accept friend requests from strangers.

4.Be Careful with Personal Information

Oversharing can make you vulnerable to harassment or stalking. Avoid sharing your home address,phone number,school or workplace location and travel plans in real time.The less personal data available publicly, the safer you are.

5.Think Before Sharing Photos or Videos

Once something is online, it can be copied or misused.
Avoid sharing sensitive images.
Do not send private content under pressure.
Be cautious of fake emotional manipulation.
Remember: Digital content can be saved even if deleted.

6.Do Not Engage with Abusers

Responding to online harassment may escalate the situation.
Do not argue.
Do not threaten back.
Do not give emotional reactions.
Instead: Block the abuser report him and keep chat histories.

7.Save Evidence Immediately

If you experience online abuse:

  • Take screenshots.
  • Save URLs.
  • Record usernames.
  • Keep chat histories.
Because evidence is important for reporting and legal action.

8.Block and Report Offenders

Most platforms have reporting tools.

  • Block the abuser.
  • Report abusive behavior.
  • Report fake accounts.
  • Report non-consensual content.
Reporting helps protect others too.Because it raise awareness among people that this has happened to someone so we should be more cautious if such type of situation occurs.

9. Secure Your Devices

  1. Install antivirus software.
  2. Keep apps updated.
  3. Avoid clicking suspicious links.
  4. Log out from shared devices.
Cyber abusers sometimes use malware or phishing.

10.Seek Support

If you are targeted, seek support because you are not alone. Many women fear being judged if they speak out about abuse, so they remain silent due to societal pressure.

Instead of hiding here is what should you do:
  1. Inform a trusted friend or family member.
  2. Speak to a teacher or counselor.
  3. Contact local cybercrime authorities.
  4. Reach out to support organizations.
Be Brave.Online abuse is serious and asking for help is a sign of strength.So show your strength and tell your trusted ones what is happening to you.

Take Action Now

Silence protects abusers and Action protects victims

So just leave the overthinking and take steps to report that abuser because the life is yours and you have to take steps for yourself even if it is against the societal norms.

Online gender-based violence is not “just online drama.” It destroys confidence. It damages mental health. It silences voices. It creates fear. Ignoring abuse allows it to continue. Taking action stops it.
If You Experience Online Abuse:

  • Stay calm and do not respond emotionally because abusers often seek reactions. Do not give them power.
  • Preserve evidence immediately and take screenshots. Save usernames. Record dates and links. Evidence is your protection.
  • Block the perpetrator and remove their access to you.
  • Report the abuse on the platform. Every report contributes to safer digital spaces.
  • Inform someone you trust. A parent, teacher, friend, or counselor.
  • Seek legal help if you are threatened because online threats and blackmail are crimes not jokes.

If You Witness Online Gender-Based Hate:

Being silent makes you complicit.You should support the victim publicaly or privately and report abusive reports.Your support to the victim at their critical time matters a lot and your silence arises a question on your humanity.
